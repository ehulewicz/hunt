# Long-form question bank

These are the base answers application prose is composed from. Each one is
written once, in your own words, and reused across applications. Write your
answer under each prompt.

How the questions were chosen: the 169 application forms in the top 500 board
listings that could be read without signing in (113 companies) were scanned,
and every long-form question was grouped by what it asks. "Asked by" below
counts companies, with listings in parentheses. Workday forms sit behind a
sign-in and were not part of the scan.

How to answer:

- Facts and numbers only from your record. Say plainly when you have no
  experience with something.
- First person, plain sentences. No semicolons, colons, or dashes inside an
  answer, the same rule drafted answers follow.
- Forms mostly ask for 2 to 5 sentences. The longest ask for 150 to 200 words
  (Palantir) or 500 words (Twitch). Write 150 to 300 words with the concrete
  details, up to 400 for a deep dive. Composing shortens. It cannot add facts.
- Do not write company-specific "why us" answers. Write the interests in
  section 1, and the posting supplies the company.

Priority: P0 answers cover the most frequent required questions. Do them
first.

## 1. Interests (P0)

"Why this company" is the most common question: asked by 15 companies (17),
required at 15. A company is matched to one of these interests. When none fits
honestly, the answer comes from technical fit instead, never from invented
enthusiasm. One combined answer per interest, 200 to 350 words, covering:

- What specifically interests you: the problems, not just the field.
- Where it started.
- How it changed as you learned more.
- What you have actually done about it: projects, courses, research, reading.
- What you would like to work on in it, and why.

## 2. Direction (P0)

### 2.1 Tell me about yourself

Who you are, what you build, what you are looking for. 150 to 250 words.

Serves: personal summary (3 companies, optional), anything else (6, optional),
introductions.

### 2.2 Why software engineering, and what kind of engineering work energizes you most

Say which you prefer and why, with evidence: backend, full stack, or ML;
customer-facing or forward-deployed work, or core product and infrastructure.

Serves: why this role or team (6), Palantir's Forward Deployed versus Software
Engineer choice, Viget's role choice, area-of-interest picks (SeatGeek,
Cloudflare, Pinterest, ZipRecruiter).

### 2.3 What are you looking for in your next internship, and what do you want to learn there

Name the skills and domains you want to grow in, and why now.

Serves: learning goals (4 companies, 7 listings: 1Password, Aurora, Viget,
Datacor), why this role, why us.

### 2.4 A technology trend you follow closely, and what you have done to learn it

Serves: industry or technology interest (3: Viget's web trend, Gemini's
Web3 interest, Palantir's "if Palantir didn't exist, what would you work on").
